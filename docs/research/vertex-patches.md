# Vertex patches at a returning notch and an unequal-radius apex

**Scope (origin/main at `912e14b4`, 2026-11-15).** This is construction advice for the round side of [ADR 0001](../adr/0001-blend-architecture.md), not a change to its semantics. The proposed [rail-misses amendment in PR #1086](https://github.com/gilbertorconde/ogeom-rs/pull/1086) is still draft, not in main. In particular, a request is one *set* of edges on the original solid, not two sequential fillet features. The note distinguishes a geometrically exact transfinite surface from the B-spline **actually stored** in the B-rep. Every containment and tangency claim below must be checked on the latter [R1, R2].

## A. Two convex rounds beside a sharp concave edge, 170°–178° turn

**Construction (estimated 1–2 weeks, including curved-face intersection and tests).** SMLib's 3×2 same-convexity rule explicitly says the two rails on their common face must be "extended and/or blended to meet" and that a corner patch closes the hole [S1]. Do not carry both bands indefinitely into a pinched overlap; PR #1086 distinguishes this case from rails which actually meet *within* the face [R1].

1. Solve both strips and their contact curves against the **same original face** `F(u,v)` in P1, including extensions beyond the crease ends. On the shared face, find every intersection of the two extended pcurves. If an admissible meeting lies inside the retained face region and has no intervening crossing, use the bands' own intersection (M5e), **not** a new connector. Otherwise choose the first admissible pair of rail endpoints before they leave that region, using a geometry/topology key, not request order. Make a single connector `c(t)` *in the face chart*, with `C(t)=F(c(t))`, endpoint positions `c(0),c(1)` and endpoint directions parallel to the incoming rail derivatives with the correct orientation. A clamped cubic spline with these Hermite data can minimize the face-metric bending energy `∫|∇_s(dc/ds)|² ds` under the constraint that it remains in the retained chart region and has no self-intersection. Fix the parametrization, knot sequence, endpoint selection, and tie-breaks canonically; do not call a straight **3D** chord on a curved face a contact curve. If the face chart crosses a seam, split and continue on the adjoining chart, verifying the same 3D curve. This minimizer is a proposed design choice, **not** a recipe supplied by SMLib; its solution needs a certified chart-domain/clearance check [S1, R1].
2. Trim/extend both bands to compatible end sections and build the transition of their face contacts to `C` **together**. Changing just the face trim without re-seating the strip can leave a position gap or destroy tangency. Derive the closed hole and its cyclic sides from the common setback graph: normally the two band-end curves and the connector on `F`; include a separate sharp-edge/face-trim side if the real hole has one. The sharp crease remains G0 across its two original faces; requiring one smooth normal *across* it is impossible. Preserve the actual three- or four-sided topology rather than forcing a triangular patch onto a quadrilateral hole [R1, R2].
3. Prefer the **existing ribbon-based Kato-style transfinite construction** (`round/transfinite.rs`) for this three-/four-sided hole, not a new generic polygon fitter. For side `i`, prescribe its exact curve `b_i(s)` and a cross-field `h_i(s)` in its supporting band/face tangent plane; use a ribbon `R_i(s,d)=b_i(s)+d h_i(s)+O(d²)` bent in the supporting surface's direction. For three sides, the implemented weights are `μ_i=∏_{j≠i} d_j² / Σ_k ∏_{j≠k} d_j²`, where `d_i` vanishes on side `i` and `s_i` follows that side. Thus `S=Σ μ_i R_i=R_i+O(d_i²)` at an open side: it shares **both position and tangent plane**, not just its boundary. Use the implementation's four-sided weights when the sharp edge supplies another side. Adjacent ribbons must agree in the *one-sided* corner tangent plane where one exists; incompatible band second fundamental forms mean no globally C1/G2 polynomial at that corner. Interpolate the exact patch into the repo's clamped B-spline only once at a fixed net; test the resulting face's residual, not the exact patch's promise [R1, R2].

**Verification/oracle.** Extend `rounds_at_a_curved_crease_turning_nearly_back_on_itself` for both edge orders at 170° and 178°: compare canonical result bits (not merely volumes), verify shared trimmed curves on the common face with a face-chart residual bound, `analyse_blend` gap and normal angle at both bands and at the open connector, no loops/inverted trims, and run `certify_face_within` on every new face against the actual kept-material region, plus `check` and no-overhang. A sample mesh and the existing **old-path** removed-volume constants are diagnostics only: the test currently records different 170°/178° answers for the two orders and explicitly says *no junction is taken*; those numbers cannot be a new-patch oracle. For a general curved boundary not represented by the certifier's plane/revolved/ball/tube half-spaces, a certified interval enclosure of the support's signed side (with trim) is needed; sampling alone cannot prove inside [R1, R3, R4]. At a face tangency of zero clearance, interval subdivision may be inconclusive: make an exact side constraint or return an honest uncertified result, never accept on a sampling threshold.

**Why not the alternatives.** A C0 multi-sided Coons patch only interpolates positions, not band tangent planes [P1]; unconstrained Bézier/B-spline G1 least-squares fitting also cannot *guarantee* those planes or inside containment (the ADR records a 0.29 overhang and twist incompatibility). Gregory/Coons–Gregory and Charrot–Gregory are viable exact-side approaches but add a different representation, corner singularity/rational-weight handling and a new proof/conversion path for today's tensor-product B-rep; there is no demonstrated advantage over the already implemented ribbons for a three-/four-sided hole. Várady–Rockwood setback splitting is a **quality knob with explicit setbacks**, not license to move the requested contacts or to make two out-of-face rails meet. The Várady–Salvi–Rockwood ribbon family is the conceptual basis of the existing method; a different transfinite evaluator alone does not solve finding the connector or certifying the *stored* face [R1, R2, P1, P2].

**Primitives.** Reuse `round/transfinite.rs` and `ogeom_algo::certify_face_within`. Add an `ogeom-algo` curved-face pcurve join/intersection with chart-seam handling, fixed endpoint tangents and proven retained-region/obstacle checks; a reusable face-metric spline optimizer may live in `ogeom-math`. A general implicit curved support requires a *new sound* interval side bound in `ogeom-algo`, unless this case is explicitly restricted to supports whose material is already expressible by `HalfSpaces` [R3].

## B. Pyramid apex, radii 0.5 / 1.5 alternating

**Construction (estimated 3–7 development days for planar pyramids; longer for nonplanar regions).** In `radius_per_edge.rs::a_pyramid_apex_at_alternating_radii_closes_with_one_patch`, four slant edges meet at `(0,0,10)` with alternate radii. Main's engine already constructs a transfinite patch, but `rounds.rs` returns `Ok(None)` when P5 runs out of subdivision without proving its face inside; the current path takes over. This is distinct from P5 **finding** an actual overhang, which is a construction error [R4, R5]. An unmerged local branch `feat/pyramid-patch-within`, commit `2605849b`, reports a fitted face that was 4.7e-3 *outside* a pyramid plane despite small band residuals, and experiments with moving non-boundary control points inward. That branch is **not** in origin/main; its reported 145 avoided old-path face pairs and tiny mesh residuals are experiment data, not a benchmark or a proof [R6].

For a convex pyramid write the original material as `M={x : n_k·(x-a_k) ≤ 0 for all slant/base planes k}` with outward **unit** normals. Represent each stored spline span as a Bézier surface (or use its nonnegative-weight B-spline controls). If every control point `P_ij` satisfies `n_k·(P_ij-a_k) ≤ ε` for every `k`, then every point of that span is within `ε` of **every** plane, by partition of unity and the convex-hull property. With rational splines this requires strictly positive weights; otherwise the argument does not apply. Include every new patch face and every convex band, not just the apex. The base plane is part of the original solid too. This is a **sufficient**, not a necessary, condition [R3].

Prescribe the band boundaries and their cross-derivative rows first. Solve for free interior control points subject to all the linear plane inequalities, minimizing deviation from the existing fitted net by a fixed strictly convex quadratic objective. A fixed active-set rule, stable edge ordering and deterministic arithmetic policy give identical results for every permutation. Never displace a boundary point; preserve a row imposing a band's G1 direction (or explicitly recheck its normal-angle bound after a constrained fit). If a fixed row violates the half-spaces, test whether a **positive** cross-derivative scale field, still in the band's tangent plane, has a feasible row. If no feasible net meeting boundary and tolerance constraints exists, do not project away a geometric contradiction: refuse with the offending plane/band. A kinked Gregory side need only be G1 on its open parameter interval, with consistent one-sided corner limits [R1–R3].

**Independent certification and oracle.** First try the fast, whole-net inequality above. If it fails, knot-insert/de Casteljau-subdivide into Bézier spans and check the same inequalities on each child net: smaller hulls may certify a valid surface without changing its geometry. An outward-rounded Bernstein/interval upper bound on `n_k·S-a_k` is also sound; for a *polynomial* Bézier patch its Bernstein coefficients are precisely the plane heights of its controls. A rational patch needs positive denominator bounds and weighted-numerator sign bounds. Interval failure means **unknown**, not inside. Existing `certify_face_within` already takes subspan hulls (depth 20, budget 2²⁰) and distinguishes `Uncertified { found: false }` from a found violation; reuse it as an independent guard. The strongest test oracle for this planar example is the four explicit slant half-spaces **and base**, checked on the **actual stored surface** with the convex-hull/interval certificate, alongside `analyse_blend`, `check`, the two permutations' bit-identical results, and the existing inequality `V(all 1.5) < V(alternating) < V(all 0.5)`. A finely triangulated check is useful only as a counterexample finder [R3–R5].

**Rejected alternatives.** Merely raising the P5 depth/budget, checking more sample points, or trusting a volume/symmetry check cannot repair an actual protrusion or prove containment at a tangent plane. A convex hull of the *boundary points alone* does not bound a free interior patch. An unconstrained Gregory, Coons–Gregory or transfinite evaluator is not inside by definition; bounding its rational pieces is more work than constraining/checking the stored positive-weight B-spline. A new setback shifts the specified contact curves without authorization. Blind control-point clamping, even if it yields containment, changes tangency and fairness unless boundary derivative constraints and the final stored face are reverified [R1–R3, R6].

**Primitives.** No new geometry **representation** is required for this planar case: `ogeom-algo::certify_face_within` and existing B-spline segmentation/positive-weight hull bounds suffice for independent proof. A reusable constrained control-net solve (linear half-spaces with fixed side rows) belongs in `ogeom-algo`; if shared arithmetic is needed, put deterministic small linear/convex solves and outward interval evaluation in `ogeom-math`. Don't use a new n-sided surface merely to get a hull: bound the B-spline the topology actually carries [R2, R3].

**Public-tool evidence, not a claim about their algorithms.** SMLib documents same-convexity NxN tangential corner blends, sometimes analytic spheres, and *multiple four-sided patches* when N>3; it does not promise the alternating-radius pyramid's shape or a containment certificate [S1]. Onshape exposes “Smooth fillet corners,” asymmetric/variable radii and “Allow edge overflow” [V1]; SOLIDWORKS documents setback/round corner parameters and Fusion distinguishes rolling-ball from setback corner types [V2, V3]. These user-facing controls show configurable corner policy, **not** the internals or a guarantee that this test will succeed. The Apache-2.0 [`solidcore` SMLib port](https://github.com/tangency-labs/solidcore) is a permitted implementation lead, but no claim here depends on its implementation [S2].

## Ranked accuracy / sound-speed opportunities elsewhere

1. **Accuracy, high confidence:** use the same curved-face connector and chart-domain certificate for other out-of-face rail ends (including self-intersecting rails); SMLib calls out both as difficult cases. Do not apply a planar-line trim to a curved support [S1, R1]. The construction benefit is predicted, not measured.
2. **Accuracy, high confidence / speed unproven:** use constrained stored-net hull proof at other *convex planar* round/setback corners; retain P5 on the actual final face. The three-sided network fan in `ogeom-algo/src/network.rs` is a different scan-fitting problem: commit `0a953c57` proves exact tangency along its curves and at its interior pole (subject to regularity). Do **not** replace it with a singular corner patch; no superiority is demonstrated [R2, R3, R7].
3. **Potential speed, correctness high / gain unmeasured:** the engine's cusp S-blend already closes with two fitted pieces of one four-sided transfinite patch (`engine/trimmed.rs`, commit `e128da40`), reportedly about 4 s. Profile before changing it. Cache the `round/transfinite.rs::interpolated_on` collocation **factorization** keyed by its exact net, knots and tolerance and use the **same** `Collocation::apply` arithmetic for each right-hand side; because the matrix is identical, the computed patch and its P5 result stay unchanged. The current code already factors **once per patch**, so only cross-patch repeated matrices are saved; a 4 s win is **not established**. Likewise reuse only *identical immutable* face/region/fit certificates, never a mesh heuristic [R2, R3, R8].
